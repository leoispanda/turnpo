## Overview

Day 3 connects financial information, enterprise risk management and governance. Read statements with judgement, identify how risk reaches objectives and make ownership, challenge and escalation visible.

## Key topics

- Three financial statements, accruals, matching, estimates and accounting judgement.
- COSO ERM: governance and culture, strategy and objectives, performance, review and revision, and information and reporting.
- Risk appetite, risk indicators, non-financial risk and the Three Lines model.
- Governance in practice, including public value, accountability and disclosure.

## Key takeaways

- Accounting numbers are evidence, but estimates, definitions and timing determine how they should be interpreted.
- ERM begins with objectives and decisions, not with an unconnected list of risks.
- Governance is a system of ownership, challenge, information and follow-up; it is not a promise that risk will disappear.
- A useful KRI has a clear owner, threshold and response.

## Self-Study

> This is a personal self-study layer. It supports independent review and classroom English; it is not an additional syllabus requirement or official course material.

### Conceptual Learning Path

Use this sequence to move from a number in a report to a management action:

1. **Information** — identify the statement, operating record, estimate or disclosure that supplies the fact.
2. **Risk identification** — ask which uncertainty could affect the organisation’s objective.
3. **Risk assessment** — test likelihood, impact, timing, dependencies and the quality of the evidence.
4. **Governance** — assign the first line owner, the second line challenge and the third line assurance.
5. **Action** — set a response, threshold, authority and deadline.
6. **Monitoring** — use a KRI and follow-up evidence to see whether the response works.

The accounting bridge matters: profit can be recognised before cash arrives, and a reported balance can have more than one operational explanation. Read the number, challenge the estimate and then trace the consequence to ownership.

### Cross-Reading Connection

**COSO → Grant Thornton → Deloitte → DSM**

- COSO connects objectives, risk appetite, performance, information and review.
- Grant Thornton turns that frame into ownership, reporting and decision processes.
- Deloitte extends the view to non-financial risks such as conduct, cyber, third parties and reputation.
- DSM tests what a company report can show about governance and risk design, while reminding us that disclosure is not proof of daily operation.

### Apply It

Choose one KPI or statement item that management sees often and answer:

1. Which financial statement or operating record produces it?
2. Which estimate, definition, timing issue or one-off event affects the interpretation?
3. Which strategic objective or public mission could it affect?
4. What KRI threshold requires action?
5. Who owns the response, who challenges it and who verifies closure?

### English Learning Support

#### Key Terms

| English | Simple meaning | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **accrual** — /əˈkruːəl/ | Recording an economic event when it occurs, not when cash moves. | Use it to explain why profit and cash can differ. |
| **matching** — /ˈmætʃɪŋ/ | Recognising costs in the period whose revenue they help create. | Check whether the expense belongs with the reported revenue. |
| **revenue recognition** — /ˈrevənjuː ˌrekəɡˈnɪʃn/ | Deciding when and at what amount revenue is recorded. | Ask what performance obligation and evidence support the entry. |
| **working capital** — /ˈwɜːkɪŋ ˈkæpɪtl/ | Operating cash tied up in receivables, inventory and payables. | Growth can consume cash when working capital expands. |
| **provision** — /prəˈvɪʒn/ | Estimate for an obligation with uncertain timing or amount. | Challenge the basis, timing and evidence behind the provision. |
| **impairment** — /ɪmˈpeəmənt/ | Recognition that an asset’s recoverable value has fallen. | Connect the accounting entry to the asset’s operating reality. |
| **governance** — /ˈɡʌvənəns/ | Arrangements for oversight, decisions, challenge and accountability. | Explain who decides, who challenges and who checks. |
| **oversight** — /ˈəʊvəsaɪt/ | Supervisory review and challenge of management. | Oversight sets direction; it does not replace daily ownership. |
| **risk appetite** — /rɪsk ˈæpətaɪt/ | Risk type and amount accepted to pursue objectives. | Turn it into limits, thresholds and actions. |
| **key risk indicator (KRI)** — /kiː rɪsk ˈɪndɪkeɪtə(r)/ | A signal that risk is accumulating before a loss. | Define the measure, owner, threshold and response. |
| **reasonable assurance** — /ˈriːznəbl əˈʃʊərəns/ | Risk reduced to a reasonable level, never to zero. | Avoid claiming that a framework guarantees no failure. |

#### Useful Ways to Say It

> “The accounting number is accurate, but the operational explanation is still uncertain.”

> “This KRI should trigger action before the loss appears in the financial statements.”

> “The first line owns the response; the second line challenges it.”

> “The report describes the control design, not yet its operating effectiveness.”
