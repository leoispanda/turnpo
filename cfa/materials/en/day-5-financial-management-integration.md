## Overview

Day 5 brings the week back to capital markets and investment products. Compare fund performance fairly by checking benchmarks, risk-factor exposure, survivorship bias and fees, then turn the course lenses into a defensible recommendation.

## Key topics

- Benchmark choice, market beta, size, value and momentum exposure.
- Alpha after risk adjustment, sample selection and survivorship bias.
- Expense ratios, management fees, carried interest and long-term fee drag.
- Integrated financial leadership: evidence, value, risk, controls and recommendation.

## Self-Study

> This is a personal self-study layer. It supports independent review and classroom English; it is not an additional syllabus requirement or official course material.

### Conceptual Learning Path

Move from an attractive performance claim to a defensible executive decision:

1. **Benchmark** — choose a comparison that matches the mandate, market, style and period.
2. **Risk factor** — separate security-selection skill from beta, size, value and momentum exposure.
3. **Sample** — ask whether failed, liquidated or excluded funds are missing from the evidence.
4. **Fee** — compare gross and net returns and let fee drag compound over the holding period.
5. **Integration** — connect value, liquidity, risk, accounting, control and stakeholder effects.
6. **Decision** — turn analysis into a recommendation, then defend the evidence gap and the condition that would change it.

### Apply It

Use the six-box memo for a real fund, project or pension choice:

1. **Decision:** What specific choice is the organisation making?
2. **Evidence and value:** Which facts and material assumptions matter, and what are the incremental cash-flow, NPV, return or fee implications?
3. **Risk:** Which exposures, downside scenarios and residual risks matter?
4. **Controls:** Which owner, KRI, boundary and review rhythm keep the decision on track?
5. **Recommendation:** What should happen now, and what evidence would change your mind?

### English Learning Support

#### Key Terms

| English | Simple meaning | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **benchmark** — /ˈbentʃmɑːk/ | Comparison standard for performance. | Match it to the mandate, risk, style and period. |
| **risk-adjusted return** — /rɪsk əˈdʒʌstɪd rɪˈtɜːn/ | Return interpreted together with the risk taken. | Do not compare return without exposure and volatility context. |
| **factor exposure** — /ˈfæktə(r) ɪkˈspəʊʒə(r)/ | Sensitivity to a common market driver. | Ask whether alpha is really beta, size, value or momentum. |
| **survivorship bias** — /səˈvaɪvəʃɪp ˈbaɪəs/ | Overstating results by observing only survivors. | Include liquidated or failed funds where possible. |
| **fee drag** — /fiː dræɡ/ | Return lost over time to fees and charges. | Compare gross performance with the investor’s net outcome. |
| **alpha** — /ˈælfə/ | Return not explained by the chosen benchmark or factors. | Treat apparent alpha as a claim to test, not a fact. |
| **material assumption** — /məˈtɪəriəl əˈsʌmpʃn/ | Assumption whose change could alter the recommendation. | Name it explicitly in the executive memo. |
| **leading indicator** — /ˈliːdɪŋ ˈɪndɪkeɪtə(r)/ | Signal that appears before loss or failure. | Use it to set a review or intervention threshold. |
| **trade-off** — /ˈtreɪd ɒf/ | Choice between objectives that cannot all be maximised. | State the trade-off instead of hiding it in a score. |
| **recommendation** — /ˌrekəmenˈdeɪʃn/ | A supported action proposed to the decision owner. | Link it to evidence, owner, timing and change condition. |

#### Useful Ways to Say It

> “The apparent alpha disappears after risk-factor and fee adjustment.”

> “The sample may be affected by survivorship bias.”

> “The recommendation depends on one material assumption about cost and implementation.”

> “My conclusion is conditional: I would change it if the evidence on downside risk worsens.”
