## Course Material

## Overview

Day 1 establishes the language for investment and financing decisions. Move from incremental cash flow and value to liquidity, financing capacity, risk management and the effect of a decision on the wider enterprise.

## Key topics

- Incremental cash flow, opportunity cost, sunk cost and NPV.
- Discounting, required return, risk-return and financial markets.
- Debt, equity, liquidity, covenants and financing capacity.
- Diversification, derivatives, hedging and core versus non-core risk.
- Enterprise and portfolio effects: when a project creates concentration risk.

## Key takeaways

- A project decision uses the cash flows that change because of the project, not accounting profit or sunk cost alone.
- Positive NPV is necessary but not sufficient: the company must survive the cash timing and financing commitments.
- Hedging should protect a defined exposure and threshold; it should not replace business judgement.
- The final recommendation should state whether to invest, stage, delay or reject, and which assumption would change that decision.

## Self-Study

> This is a personal self-study layer. It supports independent review and classroom English; it is not an additional syllabus requirement or official course material.

### Conceptual Learning Path

Use the Day 1 chain whenever a project looks attractive at first sight:

1. **Identify the change** — compare the company with and without the project and keep only the incremental cash flows.
2. **Test value** — discount the cash flows at a risk-appropriate required return and test the NPV.
3. **Test survival** — map the timing of payments, receipts, debt service, covenants and liquidity headroom.
4. **Protect the path** — hedge defined non-core exposures while retaining the risks that create the firm’s advantage.
5. **Restore the portfolio view** — check shared customers, currencies, suppliers, funding channels and correlations.
6. **Choose the decision** — invest, stage, delay or reject, with a threshold that would change the recommendation.

### Cross-Reading Connection

**Berk → Stulz → Nocco & Stulz**

- Berk provides the language of value, cash flow, discounting, financing and real options.
- Stulz explains why protecting financing capacity can create value, and why a hedge should not become a market bet.
- Nocco & Stulz extend the judgement from one project to the enterprise: look for concentration, correlation and scarce capital.

### Apply It

Choose one real investment decision and answer:

1. What decision is being made, and what is the no-project comparison?
2. Which three incremental cash flows matter most?
3. Where could liquidity or covenant pressure appear before value arrives?
4. Which risk should the company retain, and which defined exposure could it transfer?
5. What evidence or threshold would make you stage, delay or reject?

### English Learning Support

#### Key Terms

| English | Simple meaning | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **incremental cash flow** — /ˌɪnkrəˈmentl kæʃ floʊ/ | Cash changed by the decision versus not doing it. | Use it when defining the project’s real financial effect. |
| **sunk cost** — /sʌŋk kɒst/ | A past cost that cannot be recovered. | It should normally not drive today’s decision. |
| **opportunity cost** — /ˌɒpəˈtjuːnəti kɒst/ | The value of the best alternative given up. | Include the value of a facility or resource used here. |
| **net present value (NPV)** — /net ˈprezənt ˈvæljuː/ | Present value of changed cash less the initial investment. | A positive NPV supports value creation under the assumptions. |
| **discount rate** — /ˈdɪskaʊnt reɪt/ | Required return used to compare cash at different dates. | Explain why the rate matches the project’s risk. |
| **liquidity** — /lɪˈkwɪdəti/ | Ability to obtain cash and meet obligations on time. | A valuable project can still fail a liquidity test. |
| **covenant headroom** — /ˈkʌvənənt ˈhedruːm/ | Room before a debt covenant is breached. | Use it when testing financing resilience. |
| **hedge** — /hedʒ/ | Protection against a defined existing exposure. | State the exposure, amount, timing and residual risk. |
| **residual risk** — /rɪˈzɪdʒuəl rɪsk/ | Risk that remains after a response. | Report it instead of claiming that hedging removes all risk. |
| **real option** — /rɪəl ˈɒpʃn/ | Flexibility to delay, expand, contract or exit a real project. | Staged funding can preserve a better next decision. |

#### Useful Ways to Say It

> “The project creates value, but the liquidity profile is still problematic.”

> “This assumption is the main driver of the NPV.”

> “The company should retain the core risk and hedge the non-core exposure.”

> “I would stage the investment rather than approve the full commitment today.”
