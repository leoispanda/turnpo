## Assignment Guidance

BeFrank is a commercial pension provider and pension-solution partner, not a pension fund. It can still be used as the basis of this assignment. The analysis must therefore be conducted from the employer/company perspective rather than from the perspective of trustees of a pension fund.

The central question is whether impact investing makes sense for the company’s employees. If it does, the next question is whether the employer should ask BeFrank to incorporate more impact investing into its pension offering. If the existing arrangement is not suitable for that objective, the company may evaluate an alternative pension arrangement or provider.

This section is a concise synthesis of the confirmed assignment guidance. It is not a quotation from private correspondence and does not assert product capabilities that still need to be verified.

## Central Decision

> **Does impact investing make sense for the company’s employees from the employer’s perspective?**

If the answer is supported by evidence, ask whether the employer should request a stronger impact-investing option from BeFrank. If BeFrank cannot adequately support the desired outcome, compare an alternative pension arrangement only as a conditional next step.

## Confirmed Assignment Basis

The confirmed guidance is the authoritative assignment basis. It is expressed through these decision questions:

1. Does impact investing make sense for the employees covered by the company’s pension arrangement?
2. If it does, should the employer ask BeFrank to incorporate more impact investing into its pension offering?
3. If BeFrank is not the most suitable partner or structure for the objective, what alternative pension arrangement or provider setup could the company consider?

No separate fixed Q1–Q3 wording is reproduced here as an official requirement. The analysis below keeps the confirmed decision logic visible and labels the team’s own analysis separately.

## Team 6 Analysis

### 1. Employee relevance

Do not assume that impact investing is automatically desirable. Assess whether it is relevant and feasible for the employees covered by the arrangement.

Consider, where evidence is available:

* expected financial performance and risk;
* diversification and the long-term investment horizon;
* pension adequacy and the employee groups affected;
* employee preferences and sustainability objectives;
* cost, transparency and the clarity of the choice architecture;
* implementation feasibility inside the existing pension arrangement.

The evidence should come from the company’s employee context, the current pension arrangement, provider documentation and clearly identified external sources. Do not turn a preference survey into proof of a financial outcome.

### 2. Employer rationale

The employer must have a sufficiently strong reason to incorporate impact investing into its employee pension proposition. Separate the interests of employees from the employer’s own objectives and from the capabilities of the provider.

Ask:

1. What employee outcome is the employer trying to improve or protect?
2. How does the proposal affect pension design, cost, risk, choice and communication?
3. What evidence shows that the proposal is feasible and consistent with the employer’s responsibilities?
4. Which assumptions would make the proposal unattractive or impractical?

The employer case should explain why the issue matters, what decision the company controls and what it must ask the provider to demonstrate.

### 3. BeFrank suitability

If impact investing appears relevant, assess whether BeFrank is suitable for the employer’s objective. BeFrank should be assessed as a commercial pension provider or pension-arrangement partner, not as a pension fund or trustee board.

Verify current provider information before making a specific claim about investment options, employee choice, impact measurement, fees, reporting or implementation. The assessment should ask:

* Can the existing arrangement support the desired objective without weakening pension adequacy, diversification or transparency?
* What impact-investing options, if any, are currently available and how are their claims evidenced?
* What would change for employees, the employer and the administration process?
* Which costs, risks, data requirements and implementation conditions would BeFrank need to explain?

Do not invent a BeFrank product, allocation, performance promise, fee level or impact metric.

### 4. Alternative arrangement if necessary

An alternative is conditional, not the starting assumption. Consider another pension setup or provider only when the evidence shows that the existing relationship cannot adequately support the employee and employer objective.

Compare alternatives on the same basis:

| Dimension | Evidence to compare |
|---|---|
| Employee outcome | Pension adequacy, risk, choice and understandable communication |
| Investment proposition | Diversification, horizon, impact definition and verification |
| Employer feasibility | Cost, implementation effort, governance and provider accountability |
| Transparency | Reporting, fees, data ownership and escalation route |
| Transition risk | Timing, continuity, employee disruption and unresolved assumptions |

Do not recommend switching providers simply because an alternative sounds more ambitious.

### 5. Final recommendation

End at employer level. The conclusion should choose one evidence-backed category:

* retain the current arrangement;
* retain BeFrank and request stronger impact-investing options;
* adjust the pension proposition within the existing BeFrank relationship;
* consider an alternative pension arrangement or provider when the evidence shows it is necessary.

State the evidence, the remaining uncertainty, the implementation owner, the review point and the condition that would change the recommendation. The recommendation should not claim that impact investing is automatically better or that BeFrank must be replaced.

## Evidence & Analysis Boundary

Keep three authorities separate:

1. **Confirmed assignment guidance:** the employer perspective, the three decision questions, the Friday presentation and its time limit.
2. **Team 6 analysis:** research, interpretation, evidence, assumptions, comparisons and recommendation created for this case.
3. **Suggested presentation structure:** the team’s proposal for communicating the analysis within ten minutes; it is not a faculty-mandated slide count.

For every material claim, record the source, date, scope and whether it is a fact, an inference or an assumption. If a current BeFrank capability cannot be verified, say so and make the recommendation conditional.

## Suggested Presentation Structure for a 10-Minute Presentation

This is a suggested deck structure, not an official requirement and not a fixed number of slides:

1. State the employee-centred decision and the employer perspective.
2. Show the evidence for employee relevance, including the main trade-offs.
3. Explain the employer rationale and the constraints the company controls.
4. Assess BeFrank’s suitability using verified provider information.
5. Explain the conditional alternative-arrangement test.
6. Give the recommendation, implementation owner, review point and decision threshold.
7. Close with evidence limits and the strongest counterargument.
