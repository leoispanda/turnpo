# EMBA Monthly Learning Index - Corporate Finance and Accounting

## The one storyline for the week

This is not five disconnected topics. It is one executive question:

> How do I allocate capital, read the evidence, control behaviour, protect the licence to operate, and create long-term value under uncertainty?

The five Canvas entries should be managed as **five daily learning-and-delivery streams**. Even where final submission rules distinguish between chosen written answers, every stream belongs in the learning system because the frameworks reinforce one another.

| Stream | Immediate assignment focus | Management capability it builds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Financial Management | Team 6 pension-fund presentation on Impact Investing | Capital allocation, fiduciary judgment, risk/return trade-offs |
| Compliance | Heineken vs ASML principal risks and compliance-risk management | Licence to operate, governance, culture and escalation |
| Sustainability Reporting | Heineken vs ASML double materiality assessment (DMA) | ESG data governance, transparency and long-term value |
| Financial Accounting & Analysis | ASML 2025 ratios, compared with 2024 | Turning statements into a balanced financial-health judgment |
| Managerial Accounting & Control | Tennessee Controls and Simons' Levers of Control | Designing decisions and incentives without false precision |

## The five-stream work plan

The detailed checklist, output standards and preparation rhythm are in [Assignment Readiness Plan](./assignments/2026-09-five-stream-assignment-readiness-plan.md).

### 1. Financial Management — Team 6, Impact Investing

The group must advise the pension fund as a board of trustees: should it engage in Impact Investing, what are the benefits and risks, what minimum investment requirements should apply, and in which asset class impact can be most effective. The presentation must persuade a board, not merely define impact investing.

**Cross-course link:** impact targets must be evidence-based and governed; otherwise they become a sustainability-reporting claim without control, comparable data or accountability.

### 2. Compliance — risk, conduct and governance

Use the 2025 annual reports of Heineken and ASML to compare principal business-risk presentation, identify compliance-related risks, and compare their management approaches. The analytical move is from a list of risks to the operating system behind it: incentives, controls, information, escalation and board oversight.

**Cross-course link:** a compliance weakness can turn into a fine, provision, loss of trust, higher cost of capital or constrained investment capacity.

### 3. Sustainability Reporting — transparent double materiality

Use the 2024 annual reports of Heineken and ASML to compare their DMA method and presentation, then make a justified transparency judgment. Check methodology, stakeholders, impact and financial materiality, value-chain boundary, time horizons, IROs, metrics, targets, governance and assurance.

**Cross-course link:** DMA is not a reporting exercise alone. It determines which ESG facts become board information, targets, investment priorities and eventual financial risks or opportunities.

### 4. Financial Accounting & Analysis — ASML financial health

Use ASML's 2025 consolidated statements, with 2024 comparison where helpful, to analyse revenue, debt ratio, current ratio, quick ratio and return on equity; then assess further leverage from stakeholder perspectives and state what evidence is still needed.

**Cross-course link:** do not recommend leverage from a ratio alone. Link it to investment opportunities, cash-flow resilience, financing capacity, exposure to downside risk and the governance conditions that protect lenders, employees and customers.

### 5. Managerial Accounting & Control — Tennessee Controls

Explain how the Strategic Control Model can improve the formal asset-acquisition ranking process. Then show how Simons' belief, boundary and interactive systems complement its diagnostic controls and reduce the dangers of one numerical score.

**Cross-course link:** the design of ranking systems changes behaviour. A mechanically attractive project may still fail ESG, compliance, strategic-fit or learning tests.

## Core executive loop

Use this loop in class, in assignments and in work:

**Decision → Evidence → Value → Risk → Behaviour/controls → Stakeholder impact → Owner, threshold and review**

If an argument does not identify the decision owner, material evidence, trade-off and review trigger, it is still study notes rather than executive judgment.
