FINAL ASSESSMENT 
As outlined in the course syllabus, the module is assessed through two complementary 
assessment components. Together, these components evaluate all intended learning 
outcomes of the course and provide participants with the opportunity to demonstrate both 
individual analytical ability and collaborative application of financial management concepts. 
 
The assessment has been designed to evaluate your ability to apply the concepts, analytical 
frameworks and practical tools introduced throughout the module to realistic managerial 
situations. The written assessment encourages you to analyse a single organi sation from 
multiple disciplinary perspectives, while the Financial Management assessment focuses on 
the practical application of financial concepts in a collaborative setting. 
 
The assessment consists of the following components: 
 
Assessment component Weight 
Managerial Accounting & Control 25% 
Compliance or Sustainability Reporting (it is not allowed to choose both) 25% 
Financial Accounting & Analysis 25% 
Financial Management (Group Assignment & Presentation) 25% 
 
The written assessment therefore contributes  75% to the final module grade, while the 
Financial Management group assignment contributes the remaining 25%. 
 
Integrated Written Case Assignment 
 
The written assessment is built around a single company case.  The selected organisation 
provides a common organisational context through which you will analyse different 
managerial issues from complementary disciplinary perspectives. Although each question 
focuses on a specific discipline, together the questions demo nstrate how management 
accounting, financial accounting, compliance and sustainability reporting jointly contribute 
to organisational performance, responsible decision-making and long-term value creation. 
 
Each written question is assessed independently by the faculty member responsible for 
that subject area. 
 
Company Case 
 
The written assessment is largely based on ASML N.V. 
 
ASML N.V. is a Dutch multinational headquartered in Veldhoven, the Netherlands, and is 
one of the world's leading suppliers of photolithography equipment for the semiconductor 
industry. The company designs and manufactures advanced lithography machines used in 
the production of integrated circuits (microchips). ASML is unique in being the only supplier 
worldwide of Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems, a technology that is essential 
for producing the world's most advanced semiconductor chips.

MANAGERIAL ACCOUNTING AND CONTROL 
In the Tennessee Controls case study, we learned that the organisation introduced a formal 
asset acquisition process to rank competing investment proposals. Judy Starnes, the new 
division manager, is asked to rank three proposals by using techniques that q uantify 
economic returns, risk and the credibility of the individuals submitting the proposals. These 
classical financial calculations attempt to reduce each complex proposal to a single 
numerical score. While this approach can support decision-making, it may also oversimplify 
complex strategic issues and overlook factors that are difficult to quantify.  
 
a. Explain how the Strategic Control Model discussed during the course can be used 
to improve decision-making in this situation. 
b. Explain how Simons' Levers of Control (belief systems, boundary systems and 
interactive control systems) can strengthen the existing diagnostic control system 
and mitigate the disadvantages of the formal asset acquisition process. 
 
COMPLIANCE  
During the course we examined the principal business risks presented by Heineken in its 
2024 Annual Report. Using the 2025 Annual Reports of both Heineken and ASML, answer 
the following questions:  
 
a. Compare the presentation of the principal business risks by both organisations. 
b. Identify the principal similarities and differences in the risks identified. 
c. Which risks relate primarily to compliance?

a. Provide a brief analysis of ASML's revenue development. 
b. Calculate and interpret the debt ratio. 
c. Calculate and interpret the current ratio. 
d. Calculate and interpret the quick ratio. 
e. Calculate and interpret the return on equity. 
f. Assess whether further leverage would be desirable for ASML. Consider the 
perspectives of different stakeholder groups. 
g. Based on your analysis, evaluate the overall financial health of ASML and identify 
additional information you would require before making investment or strategic 
decisions.
