## PPT 页 8
Attention for sustainability is not new
In the 1970 the term ‘sustainability’ was coined by the UN.  
The ‘Brundtland rapport’ from 1987 (‘Our Common Future’) laid the foundation for the interpretation of the concept of sustainability.   
From the ’90s, companies started to report. In the beginning, the focus was on the environment (environmental reporting).
From the ’00’s, the scope broadened, and Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) became fashionable (CSR reporting).
Finally, shaping this responsibility got attention – how the process is governed.   
ESG – reporting started: Environment, Social and Governance as element of sustainability

## PPT 页 11
Reporting should drive action   
The idea is that transparency about ESG, through reporting, drives real action: companies want to show good performance
The underlying driver would be that companies that perform better, not only financially but on a broader scale, are better valued at the stock exchanges
Reporting can happen on a voluntary basis, but increases when it is mandatory
Reporting frameworks (like IFRS for financial reporting) makes non-financial reporting more comparable. Audit makes it more reliable.

## PPT 页 12
2017 – NFRD – Non-Financial Reporting Directive. Limited requirement to report on non-financial information for only the largest listed companies. No auditor involvement.
2021 – EU Taxonomy Disclosures – reporting on 6 climate targets – to what extend are company activities in line with these targets?
December 2022 – approval CSRD: Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive. Expansion of NFRD – many more reporting requirements, impacting more companies, requiring assurance from auditor.

## PPT 页 23
23
June 2026) 
Starbucks just eliminated its Chief Sustainability Officer role. No, not the person. The position is gone. We're talking about the world's largest coffee chain, which employs nearly 400,000 people, basically saying, "Sustainability doesn't matter to us." In 2020, Starbucks pledged to "build a resource positive future and cut CO2 by 50% by 2030.” In 2026, Starbucks' emissions are up by 3%, the Chief Sustainability Role is gone, lead of Reuse program - gone, and 13% more waste went to landfill. Remaining corporate sustainability team members now report to the social responsibility team... “We’re bringing sustainability and social impact under one leader because - in our coffeehouses and in coffee-growing communities - the work goes hand in hand,” says Starbucks. Translation: "The only thing Starbucks managed to cut by 50% is the sustainability team." All this, while coffee prices grew by 135% since 2016 - largely because of climate change.
