3  COURSE DESCRIPTION 
This integrated module provides MBA participants with a comprehensive foundation in 
corporate finance, accounting, performance management, enterprise risk management, 
compliance, and sustainability reporting. Together, these disciplines enable managers to 
make informed financial decisions, evaluate organisational performance, manage 
uncertainty, and create sustainable long-term value for a broad range of stakeholders. 
 
The central theme of the course is financial leadership: how managers use financial 
information, analytical tools, risk awareness, and performance management to support 
strategic decision-making in increasingly complex organisational environments. 
 
The module combines academic insights with practical applications through a balanced mix 
of recognised faculty members, experienced practitioners, guest speakers and real -world 
case studies. 
 
The course is built around four core building blocks: 
 
1. Financial Management 
This section introduces the principles of corporate financial decision -making. Participants 
explore investment appraisal, company valuation, capital structure, financing decisions, 
financial risk management, and the interaction between corporations and fin ancial 
markets. Particular attention is given to balancing risk and return while creating sustainable 
long-term value. 
 
2. Financial Accounting and Analysis 
This section develops participants' ability to interpret and evaluate financial information. 
Topics include financial statements, accounting principles, performance measurement, 
financial ratios, IFRS, and the use of accounting information to support manag erial and 
strategic decisions. 
 
3. Management Accounting and Control 
This section examines how organisations design management control systems to improve 
performance and support strategy execution. Participants explore cost management, 
budgeting, performance measurement, planning and control systems, and the evolving 
role of controllers as strategic business partners. 
 
4. Enterprise Risk Management 
Organisations increasingly operate in volatile, uncertain and highly regulated 
environments. This section introduces Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) as an integrated 
approach to identifying, assessing and managing strategic, operational, financial, 
compliance and ESG -related risks. Participants learn how effective risk management 
strengthens decision-making, organisational resilience and long-term value creation.

Building upon these foundations, the course addresses two complementary areas that have 
become indispensable for modern financial leadership. 
 
5. Compliance 
Compliance focuses on ensuring that organisations operate within legal, regulatory and 
ethical boundaries. Participants examine compliance risk, governance mechanisms, 
internal control systems, anti -corruption, data protection, financial regulation and 
organizational integrity. Rather than viewing compliance solely as a legal obligation, the 
course presents it as a strategic capability that strengthens trust, reputation and 
organisational performance. 
 
6. Sustainability Reporting 
Financial performance can no longer be evaluated independently from environmental, 
social and governance (ESG) considerations. This section introduces the principles and 
practice of sustainability reporting, including the GRI Standards, ISSB standards and the 
European Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD). Participants examine 
materiality, assurance, stakeholder expectations and integrated reporting, and learn how 
sustainability reporting contributes to transparency, accountability and long -term value 
creation. 
 
Throughout the module, Governance is addressed as a cross-cutting principle rather than 
as a separate discipline. Effective governance underpins sound financial decision -making, 
enterprise risk management, compliance, performance management and sustainability 
reporting, enabling organisat ions to balance the interests of shareholders and wider 
stakeholder groups. 
 
Together, these topics reflect the competencies expected from contemporary business 
leaders. Upon completion of the module, participants will understand how financial 
analysis, accounting information, performance management, enterprise risk management, 
compliance and sustainability reporting jointly support effective strategic decision -making 
and responsible organisational leadership. 
 
Organisational visit and guest speakers 
 
As part of the module, participants will visit the Limburg Regional Police Unit to explore 
financial management, operational control and decision-making within the public sector. In 
addition, the former CFO of DSM Asia will share practical insights into financial leadership, 
international business, strategic decision-making and leading finance functions in a global 
corporate environment. 
 
These experiences bridge theory and practice by exposing participants to diverse 
organisational settings and demonstrating how financial leadership principles are applied 
across both public and private organisations.

4  LEARNING GOALS 
This integrated module is designed to equip MBA participants with a solid foundation in the 
financial, accounting, and governance dimensions of modern business. Upon completion, 
participants will be able to: 
 
1. Understand core financial decision-making processes within corporations, including 
investment, financing, and risk management decisions, and how these decisions 
interact; 
2. Interpret and apply accounting information , both financial and managerial , for 
performance evaluation, control, and strategic decision-making; 
3. Evaluate how organisational control, enterprise risk management and compliance 
contribute to responsible financial decision -making, accountability and long -term 
value creation. 
4. Identify and assess key enterprise risks, and apply integrated risk management 
frameworks to align risk appetite with strategic objectives; 
5. Understand the role of compliance and regulation in responsible business conduct 
and risk mitigation; 
6. Analyse and interpret sustainability and ESG reporting frameworks, and assess their 
role in corporate transparency, stakeholder engagement, and long -term value 
creation; 
7. Integrate financial, accounting, performance management, risk, compliance and 
sustainability reporting insights into a holistic understanding of how organizations 
create and protect value.

7  COURSE ASSIGNMENTS 
There is no pre-assignment for this course, except from the expectation that all students 
carefully prepare the assigned literature for each session in advance. 
 
The module concludes with an integrated individual final assignment based on a central 
real-life case, which will be announced on Canvas in due time. Each core faculty member 
will formulate one question related to their area of expertise —potentially including sub -
questions or optional components —and will be responsible for evaluating your responses 
to their own question. Students will be required to sel ect and answer three questions out 
of four, subject to specific restrictions that will be communicated at the start of the 
assignment period.  Each selected question is graded separately, and carries an equal 
weight in the final grade. 
 
Further details regarding the case and set of questions will be made available on Canvas 
in due time. 
 
Assessment Criteria Per Question (=100% of Total Grade) Weight 
Criterion 1: Content – Quality of the solution to the case, including clarity 
and depth of argumentation and analysis; effective application of course 
literature; appropriate use of additional sources; and effective use of 
figures, tables, and appendices. 
 
75% 
Criterion 2: Critical Reflection – Demonstrated ability to reflect critically 
on key choices, assumptions, and limitations of the analysis and 
recommendations. Also includes the quality of documentation and 
thoughtful reflection on the use of AI. 
 
15% 
Criterion 3: Form – Structure, writing style (clear, well-organized, concise, 
and engaging), correct grammar and spelling, and formatting (including 
page numbers and an APA-style reference list at the end of each answer, 
where applicable). 
 
10% 
 
The assignment must be submitted no later than Sunday, October 25, 2026, at 23:59 
hrs. via the designated assignment submission in Canvas.  
 
Each question must be uploaded as a separate file in PDF format. Please use the following 
naming convention for each file: LastName_FirstName_StudentID_QuestionX.pdf 
Failure to follow these guidelines may result in a grade penalty.
