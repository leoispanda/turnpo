# Five-Stream Assignment Readiness Plan

## Operating principle

Treat each day as a small executive work cycle, not as a reading marathon:

1. **Before class:** arrive with one provisional answer, two pieces of evidence and one question.
2. **In class:** record only what changes your current answer, evidence standard or decision rule.
3. **After class:** turn the material into one visible output within 20 minutes while the discussion is fresh.

Use the same six-line evidence card for every stream:

> Claim | Evidence and page number | Course lens | Trade-off | Decision owner | What would change my view?

This prevents a familiar EMBA failure mode: producing long notes without a position, evidence or action.

## Stream 1 — Financial Management / Group presentation

**Team:** 6 — Marjaan Kempen, Birgitta Nielsen, Leo (Zhen) Yang, Jonathan Zart.

**Case:** Pension fund and Impact Investing.
**Assessment:** group assignment and classroom presentation; 25% of the module grade; due 11 September 2026 at 23:59 according to Canvas.

### Board-ready recommendation structure

1. **Decision:** the pension fund should adopt a measured impact-investing allocation, not a broad label-based commitment.
2. **Why:** it can support long-term beneficiaries while targeting risk-adjusted, net-of-fee returns and maintaining diversification, liquidity and fiduciary discipline.
3. **Guardrails:** measurable theory of change; additionality and intentionality; independent impact measurement; transparent costs; risk/return benchmark; liquidity and concentration limits; credible manager governance; periodic review.
4. **Asset-class choice:** compare listed equities, green/social bonds, private equity/private debt, infrastructure and real assets. Do not declare one universally best—choose based on measurable impact, liquidity needs, fees, manager skill and the fund's age profile.
5. **Recommendation:** start with a defined pilot allocation and a review date, then scale only if both financial and impact evidence meet the pre-set threshold.

### What Leo should prepare before the group meeting

- Bring a one-page view of the fiduciary trade-off: beneficiaries' retirement adequacy, return, risk, costs, liquidity and sustainability preferences.
- Propose a single slide: **“Impact is a governed investment strategy, not a marketing category.”**
- Ask the group to decide the company/pension fund first; do not build a generic deck before a real fund's participant profile, current policy and public disclosures are known.
- Prepare three hostile board questions: “Will returns fall?”, “How will we prove impact?”, “What happens if an illiquid fund needs cash?”

### High-value class notes to capture

- The teacher's definition of fiduciary duty and the distinction between responsible and impact investing.
- Any required investment framework, return benchmark, risk constraint or Dutch pension context.
- The specific evidence a board would accept before changing strategic asset allocation.

## Stream 2 — Compliance

**Case:** compare Heineken and ASML 2025 annual reports.

### Best answer architecture

| Section | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Presentation | Compare location, grouping, risk taxonomy, likelihood/impact language, trend indicators, ownership and linkage to strategy. |
| Risks | Separate shared risks (regulation, cyber, supply chain, integrity) from sector-specific risks (alcohol regulation/marketing vs export controls/technology/geopolitics). |
| Compliance | Define it operationally: legal and regulatory obligations, conduct, export/competition/anti-bribery/data/AML where relevant. |
| Management | Compare policies with evidence of training, monitoring, investigations, reporting channels, audit, remediation and board oversight. |
| Explanation | Explain differences through business model, geography, regulation, supply chain, technology and stakeholder exposure—not through generic claims about “better governance.” |

### Preparation checklist

- Create a two-company risk matrix with a precise report page for every claim.
- Mark risks as financial, operational, strategic, compliance or mixed; explain overlaps rather than forcing one label.
- Use the COSO lens: governance/culture, strategy/objectives, performance, review/revision, information/reporting.
- Finish with one limitation: annual reports show disclosed design and selected outcomes; they cannot by themselves prove day-to-day control effectiveness.

## Stream 3 — Sustainability Reporting

**Case:** compare the 2024 annual reports of Heineken and ASML on double materiality analysis (DMA).

### DMA comparison grid

| Test | Evidence to find in both reports |
| --- | --- |
| Method | Process steps, stakeholder inputs, internal governance and validation |
| Two lenses | Clear separation and connection between impact materiality and financial materiality |
| Scope | Operations, value chain, affected stakeholders and time horizons |
| Result | Named impacts, risks and opportunities (IROs), threshold or prioritisation logic |
| Decision usefulness | Metrics, baselines, targets, financial effects, owners and links to strategy/capital allocation |
| Transparency | Method limits, changes in approach, data gaps and assurance status |

### Strong conclusion rule

Do not call a report transparent because it has more ESG pages or better design. Call it more transparent only if a reader can reconstruct **how** material topics were chosen, **where** they occur, **who** owns them and **how** performance will be tested.

### Preparation checklist

- Start with the reports' DMA method and results pages before reading the whole sustainability section.
- Keep a comparison table; do not write two isolated company summaries.
- Use course terms precisely: DMA, IRO, value chain, impact materiality, financial materiality, ESRS and assurance.
- State one reasonable counterargument to your transparency conclusion.

## Stream 4 — Financial Accounting & Analysis

**Case:** ASML 2025 consolidated financial statements with 2024 comparison where appropriate.

### Calculation sheet: make the numerator and denominator visible

| Required analysis | Formula / check |
| --- | --- |
| Revenue development | 2025 revenue, 2024 revenue, absolute and percentage change; identify mix, geography or timing drivers from management discussion. |
| Debt ratio | Use the exact definition taught in class. If none is prescribed, state the formula explicitly—for example total liabilities ÷ total assets—and use it consistently across both years. |
| Current ratio | Current assets ÷ current liabilities. |
| Quick ratio | State the course definition; commonly (cash + short-term investments + receivables) ÷ current liabilities, or current assets less inventories ÷ current liabilities. |
| Return on equity | Net income ÷ average shareholders' equity; disclose if a different course convention is used. |

### Interpretation discipline

Every ratio needs three sentences:

1. **What changed?** Give the calculation and comparable prior-year figure.
2. **Why might it have changed?** Cite the note or business development, not intuition.
3. **Why does it matter?** Connect it to liquidity, leverage, profitability, financing capacity or stakeholder risk.

### Leverage recommendation

Do not answer “more leverage is good/bad.” State conditions. ASML may use additional debt only if expected investment returns exceed its risk-adjusted cost, liquidity headroom remains robust, cyclical demand and customer concentration are manageable, and the added obligation does not weaken strategic flexibility. Address equity holders, lenders, employees, customers, suppliers and governments separately where their concerns differ.

### Additional evidence to request

- debt maturity and interest-rate profile;
- undrawn facilities and liquidity stress testing;
- order backlog, customer concentration and cancellation exposure;
- capex/R&D commitments and working-capital cycle;
- export-control/geopolitical scenarios;
- off-balance-sheet commitments and contingent liabilities.

## Stream 5 — Managerial Accounting & Control

**Case:** Tennessee Controls and its formal asset-acquisition ranking process.

### Answer map

| Prompt | Answer move |
| --- | --- |
| Strategic Control Model | Place ranking inside a wider cycle: strategic assumptions and objectives → project evidence → resource allocation → implementation feedback → strategic review. Use the model to expose uncertainties, interactions and learning, rather than merely calculate a score. |
| Diagnostic control | Keep the formal ranking process for comparability, budget discipline and tracking against plans. |
| Belief system | Clarify purpose and strategic priorities so managers know what value creation the organisation is seeking beyond score maximisation. |
| Boundary system | Set non-negotiable limits—ethics, safety, compliance, capital-at-risk, strategic fit—so an attractive numerical score cannot approve an unacceptable project. |
| Interactive control | Use regular senior-management dialogue around strategic uncertainties, assumptions, competitor/technology changes and project learning; revise or stage decisions as evidence develops. |

### Critical reflection that earns marks

A multi-factor ranking process improves consistency but may create false precision. It can hide subjective weights, double count risks, reward persuasive proposers and understate strategic complementarity or real-option value. The answer is not to abandon numbers: retain the diagnostic screen, add qualitative challenge, set boundaries, and make high-uncertainty investments subject to staged funding and interactive review.

| Evening before | Read the day's assignment prompt and make one evidence card per core source. | 45–60 min |
| Before class | Write one question whose answer would improve your eventual recommendation. | 5 min |
| During class | Capture only corrections to your claim, evidence or method. | — |
| Same evening | Update the relevant table, calculation sheet or slide outline. | 20–30 min |
| Friday | Convert each stream into a one-page executive summary; identify gaps and owners. | 60–90 min |

## Optimisation tips for learning and grades

1. **Work backwards from the deliverable.** Read a source to improve one sentence, comparison row, calculation or slide—not to “finish” it.
2. **Use comparison before description.** For Heineken/ASML, write every paragraph as a similarity, difference, explanation or judgment; this is sharper than two company summaries.
3. **Separate fact, inference and recommendation.** Annual report facts need page references; your inference needs a logical bridge; recommendations need a condition and owner.
4. **Use one shared evidence bank.** ASML's risk governance, capital position and ESG disclosures may support different assignments; record the original report page once and reuse it carefully.
5. **Make calculations auditable.** Keep an input table, formulas, year labels and source page. Round only in the final presentation, never in your working sheet.
6. **Practice the board test.** After every conclusion, ask: “What would a sceptical director challenge, and which evidence would I show?”
7. **Use AI as a challenger, not a source.** It can propose structure, test counterarguments and edit language. Verify every number, annual-report claim and citation in the original source; retain a short log of what you accepted, rejected and checked.
8. **Protect sleep before class.** The highest-value work happens when you can follow argument, challenge assumptions and contribute to group discussion; do not trade that for late-night note accumulation.

## Submission-control list

- Keep each written answer within the stated two-page maximum unless the instructor formally changes it.
- Keep a separate working folder for each stream: `sources`, `evidence table`, `draft`, `final PDF`.
- Use APA references and make every factual claim traceable.
- Before upload, check the required filename pattern: `LastName_FirstName_StudentID_QuestionX.pdf`.
- Canvas language provided in the assignment pages contains an apparent tension between “select and answer three” and “answer all four.” Keep all five learning streams active, and confirm the final written-upload count with the course instructor or Canvas clarification before submission.
