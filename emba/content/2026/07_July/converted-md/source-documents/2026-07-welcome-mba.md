# Welcome MBA - Ron Jacobs

## 1. One-Line Summary

The welcome deck frames MaastrichtMBA as a diverse professional learning community built around co-creation, feedback, networking, participant responsibility, and problem-based learning.

## 2. Programme Community

The deck introduces the MBA management team, programme structure, student presidents, and the relationship between programme management, faculty, advice and regulation, and the student body.

The community expectations are summarized through:

- direction;
- co-creation;
- networking;
- feedback;
- ambassadorship.

Indicative student-body figures in the source are about 200 students, 50 nationalities, an average age of 40, and 15 years of work experience. The deck explicitly says these figures change as students join and graduate.

## 3. Learning Through Diversity

The behavioural invitation is to be open, be curious, delay judgment, be thoughtful, and keep the programme informed. Diversity is presented as a learning mechanism rather than only a demographic fact.

The deck contrasts formal and informal learning and asks how participants will use the next phase of their professional life: reflect, prioritize, get inspired, explore, and make friends.

## 4. Institutional Context

The programme is situated inside:

1. Maastricht city.
2. Maastricht University.
3. School of Business and Economics.
4. UMIO, the professional-development branch.
5. MaastrichtMBA.

The source provides background figures for Maastricht University and describes UMIO's executive MBA, executive master, and executive-education portfolio. Treat all institutional counts as source-time context rather than permanent facts.

## 5. Problem-Based Learning

The deck contrasts two learning sequences.

Traditional sequence:

1. The teacher defines what students need to know.
2. Learning proceeds from theory into application.
3. A case later demonstrates use of that knowledge.

Problem-Based Learning sequence:

1. A case or problem is presented.
2. Learners define their own learning objectives.
3. Theory is applied to analyse and solve the problem.

This supports Leo's T01 idea that a real problem can be the entry point to learning.

## 6. Learning Pyramid Boundary

One slide presents fixed learning-retention percentages for lecture, reading, audio/visual, demonstration, discussion, practice, and teaching others.

Evidence boundary: the slide is part of the classroom deck, but the fixed percentages commonly associated with a "learning pyramid" should not be treated as established evidence without an authoritative research source. The safe takeaway is that active practice, discussion, and teaching can create different learning conditions than passive exposure; the exact percentages remain unverified here.
