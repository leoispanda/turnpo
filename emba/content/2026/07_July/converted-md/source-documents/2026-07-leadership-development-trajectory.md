# Leadership Development Trajectory - Micole Smits

## 1. One-Line Summary

The Leadership Development Trajectory is presented as a participant-owned process for turning real leadership experience into self-awareness, behavioural experiments, feedback, and sustained change at work.

## 2. Source Role

This 24-page slide deck introduces the formal LDT architecture. It is the course source for programme design and classroom models; it is not Leo's personal conclusion or evidence that a behavioural change has already occurred.

Use the protected PDF for the original diagrams and exact slide wording. Use this Markdown for search and future RAG.

## 3. Purpose And Ownership

The stated goal is to help participants cope better with personal leadership and the leadership of others, with or without formal authority.

The trajectory is described as:

- integrated into the MBA curriculum;
- customized to the participant;
- designed to stimulate self-awareness and skills development;
- structured to enable change;
- intended to transfer learning into the work environment.

Ownership stays with the participant. The deck asks each person to define:

- the depth of self-reflection and development;
- the skills and competencies to develop;
- the kind of leader they want to become;
- how they want to lead self and others;
- how leadership development fits daily physical, mental, and emotional life.

## 4. Experiential Learning Cycle

The deck uses Kolb's learning cycle:

1. Concrete experience.
2. Observation and reflection.
3. Forming abstract concepts.
4. Testing in new situations.

Its practical version is: notice experience, build self-awareness, adjust limiting thought patterns, test alternative behaviour, analyse effects, and set the next learning goal.

This cycle is important for Leo's Reflection system because a thought is not complete when it is written down. It becomes learning only after a behavioural test and a later review.

## 5. LDT Support Structure

| Component | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Questionnaire | Self-reflection, personal inspiration, responsiveness, vitality, leadership of others, leadership attitude, and open questions |
| Individual coaching | Intake plus six sessions |
| Buddy coaching | Five to six sessions |
| Mutual Learning Groups | Six sessions |
| Modular-week training | Leadership practice inside the educational weeks |
| Online-module teamwork | Team learning and application |
| Knowledge | Conceptual input supporting practice |
| Reflection report | Mid-term and final personal narrative |

The questionnaire includes self-view and 360-degree perspectives on collaboration, results, control, creativity, learning, response ability, and responsibility.

## 6. Leadership And Sustainability

The deck connects the UN Sustainable Development Goals with the Inner Development Goals. It argues that implementation also depends on inner capabilities rather than policy alone, and introduces five IDG dimensions:

- Being: relationship to self.
- Thinking: cognitive skills.
- Relating: caring for others and the world.
- Collaborating: social skills.
- Acting: enabling change.

Evidence boundary: these are frameworks presented by the course. Claims about the number of contributors or the main causes of implementation failure should be verified against current IDG source material before external use.

## 7. Listening Practice

The coaching exercise asks participants to notice:

- how long they listen without asking questions;
- what kinds of questions they ask;
- how quickly they give advice;
- whether a closed question is actually advice disguised as inquiry.

The deck presents four levels of listening, attributed to C. Otto Scharmer's Theory U:

1. Downloading from habits and reconfirming old judgments.
2. Factual listening with an open mind and attention to disconfirming data.
3. Empathic listening with an open heart and another person's perspective.
4. Generative listening with open will and attention to an emerging future.

It also names three voices of resistance: judgment, cynicism, and fear.

## 8. Reflection Exercises

The closing exercises ask for two real leadership situations:

- one in which the participant was proud and effective;
- one in which the participant was dissatisfied and had to learn.

In pairs, the listener identifies the values important to the speaker and what the speaker had to let go of in order to learn.
